# Design language

## Intent

Seed Protocol should feel like a durable research instrument: the clarity of a 1994 laboratory terminal with the humane navigation of a thoughtful 2035 interface.

The identity is independent from Rainier Dynamics. It uses no inherited corporate logo, palette, or marketing language.

## Principles

### Monochrome first

Every page must work in GitHub light mode, dark mode, print, plain Markdown, and copied text. Color may clarify status later but must never carry meaning alone.

### Navigation before decoration

Use descriptive links, compact route tables, explicit start paths, and predictable page endings. Avoid ornamental banners, stock imagery, excessive badges, and decorative emoji systems.

### Research status, not product theater

Prefer labels such as `S2 · E1 · 0.1-draft · public experiment`. Do not use claims like “revolutionary,” “production-ready,” or “the future of knowledge.”

### Terminal restraint

Short monospace blocks may show state, flow, commands, or object grammar. They should not make ordinary prose harder to read.

### Progressive disclosure

| Time | Reader outcome |
|---|---|
| 30 seconds | Curiosity and an accurate one-sentence explanation |
| 3 minutes | Understanding of the problem, Seed object, and experimental status |
| 10 minutes | A route into Canon, attack, Quest, or agent boot |
| 20 minutes | A contribution path appropriate to the reader |

### Honest uncertainty

Every persuasive claim should remain adjacent to its evidence state. Experimental status and serious objections must be visible from the public front door.

## Page grammar

Most public pages should contain:

1. a plain-language purpose;
2. what the reader will get;
3. the smallest useful explanation;
4. deeper paths;
5. current uncertainty or limits;
6. one clear next action.

## Diagrams

Prefer Mermaid for relationships and short text alternatives immediately below it. Diagrams must simplify a relationship that prose alone makes harder to see.

## Terminology

Capitalize defined protocol objects such as Seed, Canon, Workspace, Decision, Challenge, and Experiment when the formal meaning matters. Explain the term on first use for newcomers.

## Accessibility

- Use descriptive link text rather than “click here.”
- Include text alternatives for every Mermaid diagram.
- Avoid tables containing long narrative passages.
- Keep headings hierarchical.
- Avoid meaning conveyed only by position, color, or icon.
- Prefer direct language and short paragraphs.
- Explain GitHub-specific words in contribution guidance.

## Future visual assets

Any later mark or illustration should remain optional, original, legible at small sizes, printable in one color, and nonessential to understanding the repository.
