# C-0003 — Gamification may corrupt the contribution culture

**Status:** open  
**Target:** P-16 through P-20  
**Severity:** high

## Claim

Even well-intended quests, scores, roles, bounties, and recognition can shift attention from useful truth-seeking toward visible, rewarded behavior. A system that begins by matching people to purpose may end by manufacturing engagement.

## Attack

- Bounties favor legible short-term questions over quiet long-term stewardship.
- Prestige for falsification produces performative contrarianism.
- Domain reputation entrenches incumbents and discourages newcomers.
- Agent reward functions optimize evaluation artifacts rather than reality.
- “Safe” progression still creates status competition.

## Proposed test

Run early collaboration with no points, levels, streaks, or global leaderboard. Use only explicit questions, voluntary quests, peer acknowledgment, and outcome review. Add mechanics individually and measure contribution quality and participant autonomy.

## Reversal condition

If motivation primitives consistently degrade epistemic quality or participant well-being, remove them from the protocol core and treat them solely as optional application experiments.
