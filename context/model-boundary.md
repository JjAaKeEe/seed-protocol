# Model boundary

Seed Protocol deliberately models only part of the reality surrounding ideas, collaboration, knowledge, motivation, and machine agency.

## What the current model includes

- structured capture and handoff of an idea;
- separation of accepted state, exploration, evidence, and history;
- maturity and machine-executability dimensions;
- deterministic agent orientation;
- questions, Decisions, Challenges, Experiments, and failures;
- provenance and attribution as design requirements;
- adversarial testing and reversal conditions;
- high-level motivation and contribution matching;
- high-level machine authority boundaries;
- a plain-file reference implementation.

## What it does not currently model well

- legal ownership of jointly created ideas;
- privacy-preserving provenance;
- cultural differences in authorship, authority, and disagreement;
- conflict resolution between contributors;
- community governance under adversarial participation;
- archival authenticity and tamper evidence;
- long-term identity of people or agents;
- economic incentives and funding;
- formal epistemology or probabilistic belief revision;
- malicious Seeds or dual-use content;
- security of agent tool execution;
- permission delegation and revocation;
- physical-system safety cases;
- accessibility and multilingual use;
- offline-first collaboration beyond file portability;
- emotional and social context lost during distillation;
- maintenance cost at large scale.

## Known biases in origin

The concept originated through an extended English-language conversation between one human and AI assistants, with a systems-engineering and software metaphor set. It may overvalue explicit documentation, modularity, formal state, and repository-like workflows. It may undervalue oral tradition, tacit knowledge, embodied apprenticeship, informal trust, indigenous knowledge practices, and cultures that do not separate authorship or Canon in the same way.

The reference implementation is being built in a coding environment, which may make files and command-line tooling appear more universal than they are.

## Current evidence quality

The conceptual framework is internally developed but not externally validated. Claims about collaboration, motivation, long-term durability, agent reliability, and cross-platform interoperability are hypotheses. The current implementation proves only that the proposed artifacts can be represented and checked locally.

## Unknown unknowns

The system cannot enumerate categories it lacks. It therefore prefers reversible choices, open formats, explicit uncertainty, optional layers, and periodic ontological challenge.

## Evidence that would weaken the core thesis

- independent participants consistently require the originator despite complete Seeds;
- bootload artifacts create more confusion than direct narrative reading;
- the common structure substantially distorts unrelated Seed types;
- decision/challenge records cost more than the repeated error they prevent;
- platform-independent files cannot preserve the state needed for useful agent participation;
- long-dormant Seeds prove practically unrecoverable despite following the protocol.

## Evidence that would strengthen it

- unfamiliar humans and agents orient accurately with little assistance;
- contributors resolve useful questions without joining the project;
- a Seed survives years of dormancy and reactivates cheaply;
- derivatives preserve ancestry while departing freely;
- challenges catch costly errors before implementation;
- multiple platforms interoperate from the same corpus;
- implementations retain human intent while increasing machine capability.
