# Authority and agency boundary

## Core rule

> Knowledge of a Seed does not confer authority over the Seed, its contributors, external systems, money, machines, or the physical world.

The manifest records intended policy but cannot itself grant permission. Actual authority comes from the current user, owner, institution, operating environment, law, and applicable safety controls.

## Default posture for S-0000

| Action | Default |
|---|---|
| Read and summarize local Seed files | Allowed |
| Analyze and critique locally | Allowed |
| Propose Questions, Challenges, Decisions, or Experiments | Allowed |
| Run safe local tests in an approved sandbox | Task-dependent |
| Change Canon | Review required |
| Publish or message externally | Explicit approval required |
| Choose public licenses | Explicit owner decision required |
| Spend money or create paid services | Explicit approval required |
| Fabricate artifacts | Explicit approval required |
| Operate instruments or physical systems | Explicit approval and safety controls required |

## Non-inheritance

Permission at one level does not imply permission at a higher-consequence level:

- research does not authorize outreach;
- simulation does not authorize experimentation;
- experimentation does not authorize fabrication;
- fabrication does not authorize deployment;
- deployment does not authorize autonomous operation;
- access to a tool does not imply permission to use it for every Seed objective.

## Higher executability requirements

Before E4–E6 work, a Seed should eventually record:

- responsible human or institution;
- permitted tools and targets;
- environment and containment;
- budget and rate limits;
- approval gates;
- audit trail;
- hazard analysis;
- stop conditions;
- emergency shutdown;
- recovery and incident reporting;
- permission expiry and revocation.

These are research requirements, not yet a complete safety specification.
