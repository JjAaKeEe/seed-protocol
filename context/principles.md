# Constitutional principles

These principles govern Seed Protocol more strongly than any current file layout. Implementations may change quickly; constitutional principles should change deliberately through recorded Decisions.

## P-01 — Human readability

A capable human should be able to understand the essential Seed without proprietary software.

## P-02 — Agent bootability

An unfamiliar capable agent should be able to orient itself deterministically without prior chat history or creator memory.

## P-03 — Explicit Canon

The authoritative working state must be identifiable. Canon coordinates work; it does not claim infallibility.

## P-04 — Platform independence

The protocol must not depend on GitHub, Git, a specific AI vendor, one database, or one application.

## P-05 — Provenance persists

Important ideas retain their intellectual ancestry, relevant evidence, and meaningful contributors.

## P-06 — Uncertainty remains visible

Observation, evidence, inference, assumption, hypothesis, preference, and fiction must not silently collapse into one another.

## P-07 — Dormancy is legitimate

A Seed may sleep indefinitely without being considered failed. Reactivation should not require reconstructing its history from memory.

## P-08 — Forking is healthy

Fundamental disagreement may produce a derivative Seed. The original creator does not own the conceptual future.

## P-09 — Contribution is modular

Someone may contribute one useful observation without joining a project or community.

## P-10 — Transferability

The Seed must be capable of surviving its creator, steward, preferred model, and original platform.

## P-11 — Layered context

Participants should load the minimum trustworthy context required for the task, with deeper material retrievable as needed.

## P-12 — Interoperability over lock-in

Stable interfaces and open representations are more durable than applications.

## P-13 — Seeds need not become projects

Preserving a worthwhile idea is itself a legitimate outcome.

## P-14 — History has three forms

Preserve version history, decision rationale, and intellectual provenance as distinct but connected records.

## P-15 — Dogfood the protocol

Seed Protocol and any future Seed Commons must be developed as Seeds.

## P-16 — Motivated participation

Expose meaningful, appropriately difficult opportunities that support autonomy, mastery, curiosity, relatedness, and purpose.

## P-17 — Productive adversarialism

Reward challenge, falsification, replication, correction, and alternative models at least as strongly as confirmation.

## P-18 — Contribution over activity

Reputation must not derive primarily from posting, frequency, time spent, or output volume.

## P-19 — Purpose matching

Help humans and agents discover problems suited to their expertise, interests, available effort, location, and desired challenge.

## P-20 — Safe motivation

Do not depend on deception, exploitative variable rewards, artificial scarcity, streak anxiety, or compulsive engagement.

## P-21 — Assumed incompleteness

Every Seed begins from the assumption that its model captures only a fraction of relevant reality.

## P-22 — Adversarial by default

Mature Seeds should be continuously subjected to structured attack, edge cases, red teams, and alternative-model comparison.

## P-23 — Reward correction

Discovering that a Seed is wrong can be more valuable than extending it.

## P-24 — Preserve failure

Failed experiments, rejected hypotheses, and invalidated Decisions remain durable knowledge when they prevent repeated error.

## P-25 — Reversible Canon

Major Decisions should state the conditions or evidence that would cause reconsideration.

## P-26 — Ontological challenge

The system must periodically challenge the categories and assumptions used to define the problem, not only the conclusions.

## P-27 — Stress generates knowledge

A Seed should become more useful through criticism, changed conditions, edge cases, and unsuccessful experiments.

## Operational corollaries

- Conversation is input, not authoritative state.
- Knowledge does not imply permission.
- Validation checks structural coherence, not truth.
- A polished document can still be S1.
- Ten agreeing agents do not transform inference into fact.
- The best handoff reduces dependence on the participant who created it.
- Simplicity is not minimal file count; it is minimal conceptual burden for reliable transfer.
